Phase 1 · Core Mechanics
How Japanese Works
Japanese is wired differently from English. Understanding these fundamentals will make everything else click.
Sentence Structure: Subject → Object → Verb

English: I eat sushi. (S → V → O)

Japanese: 私は寿司を食べます。 (S → O → V)

Watashi wa sushi wo tabemasu.

The verb always comes last. Once you internalize this, Japanese sentences start to make sense.

Particles — The Grammar Glue

Particles are tiny words that show the role of each word in a sentence. They're unique to Japanese and take a little getting used to — but there are only a handful of essential ones.

Politeness Levels

Japanese has two main registers. You'll start with polite form — it's safe everywhere.

✓ Polite Form (masu/desu)
Use with strangers, shop staff, service workers, and anyone you've just met. Safe everywhere.
食べます — tabemasu (eat)
学生です — gakusei desu (am a student)
Plain Form (dictionary/casual)
Use with close friends, family, and peers. Also used in anime and games — so you'll recognize it even if you don't speak it yet.
食べる — taberu (eat)
学生だ — gakusei da (am a student)
Questions — Just Add か

To turn any statement into a question, add か (ka) at the end. No word order change needed.

これは寿司です。→ This is sushi.
これは寿司ですか？→ Is this sushi?
Kore wa sushi desu ka?

Numbers 数字
Numbers are essential from day one — for prices, floors, train times, and counting. The good news: Japanese numbers follow a perfectly logical pattern.
1 – 10 (The Core)
How to Build Larger Numbers

Japanese numbers are perfectly logical — no irregular forms like "eleven" or "twelve".

11 = 十一 (juu ichi) — literally "ten one"
20 = 二十 (ni juu) — literally "two ten"
35 = 三十五 (san juu go) — "three ten five"
100 = 百 (hyaku)
1,000 = 千 (sen)
10,000 = 万 (man) — Japanese groups by 10,000, not 1,000
💡 Price tip ¥1,500 = 千五百円 (sen go hyaku en). Read the digits, apply the place values. Once you've practiced, prices click fast.
