# Japanese Language Tutor — Project Outline

**Goal:** A context-driven Japanese tutor for a beginner, focused on travel/daily life and anime/games. Unlike Duolingo-style apps, every lesson is anchored to a real scenario so nothing feels abstract or out of context.

**Learner level:** Beginner  
**Target contexts:** Travel & everyday life | Anime, manga & games

---

## Phase 1: Foundation (Before Any Scenarios)

Get the minimum viable tools to function in Japanese.

### 1.1 Writing Systems
- Hiragana (46 characters) — reading and writing
- Katakana (46 characters) — reading and writing
- Kanji introduction — what it is, how to approach it gradually
- Romaji — when to use it (sparingly) and when to move past it

### 1.2 Core Language Mechanics
- Sentence structure: Subject → Object → Verb (SOV)
- Particles: は (wa), が (ga), を (wo), に (ni), で (de), の (no)
- Politeness levels: plain vs. polite (masu/desu forms)
- Yes/No, question formation (か)
- Numbers 1–10,000 (essential for prices, dates, transit)

## Phase 2: Real-World Track (Travel & Daily Life)

Each unit is a **scenario**. Every vocab, grammar point, and phrase is taught because it matters in that situation.

### Unit 1 — Arriving in Japan
- Scenario: At the airport, through customs, finding your hotel
- Key vocab: passport, luggage, taxi, hotel, exit, gate
- Grammar: asking where things are (〜はどこですか？)
- Culture note: bowing, politeness norms on arrival

### Unit 2 — Getting Around
- Scenario: Trains, buses, asking for directions
- Key vocab: station, ticket, platform, left/right/straight, map
- Grammar: 〜まで (until/to), 〜に乗る (to ride/board)
- Culture note: IC cards (Suica/Pasmo), quiet car etiquette

### Unit 3 — Food & Restaurants
- Scenario: Entering a restaurant, ordering, paying
- Key vocab: menu, recommendation, allergy, bill, delicious
- Grammar: 〜をください (please give me), 〜はありますか？ (do you have?)
- Culture note: no tipping, how to call a waiter, set meals (定食)

### Unit 4 — Shopping
- Scenario: Convenience stores, markets, asking prices
- Key vocab: price, discount, size, color, bag, receipt
- Grammar: 〜はいくらですか？ (how much is?), 〜をもらえますか？
- Culture note: konbini culture, cashless payment options

### Unit 5 — Small Talk & Meeting People
- Scenario: Introductions, casual conversation with locals
- Key vocab: name, country, hobby, work, weather
- Grammar: 〜と申します (formal introduction), 〜が好きです (I like)
- Culture note: exchanging business cards, avoiding personal questions

### Unit 6 — Emergencies & Problem-Solving
- Scenario: Lost, sick, something stolen, missed train
- Key vocab: help, hospital, police, lost item, emergency
- Grammar: 〜てください (please do), 〜がわかりません (I don't understand)
- Culture note: 110 (police) vs. 119 (ambulance), tourist helplines

## Phase 3: Media Track (Anime, Manga & Games)

Anime/game Japanese is its own dialect — casual, gendered, full of slang. This track teaches it in context.

### Unit 1 — Casual vs. Polite Speech
- Why anime sounds different from textbook Japanese
- Plain form (だ、じゃない) vs. polite form (です、ます)
- When each is used in real life

### Unit 2 — Emotions & Reactions
- Scenario: Understanding emotional scenes and exclamations
- Key expressions: やばい、すごい、なるほど、うそ、え？、マジで？
- Grammar: sentence-ending particles よ、ね、な、ぞ、ぜ
- Common expressions that don't translate literally

### Unit 3 — Character Archetypes & Speech Patterns
- The hero (energetic, informal), the mentor (formal, wise), the villain (dramatic)
- Gendered speech: 俺/僕 (ore/boku) vs. あたし/わたし
- Honorifics in use: さん、くん、ちゃん、先輩 (senpai)、先生

### Unit 4 — Game & Action Vocabulary
- Scenario: Understanding RPGs, fighting games, strategy games
- Key vocab: attack, defend, level up, quest, save, inventory, skill
- Commands and menus in Japanese games
- Reading katakana loanwords (most game terms are katakana)

### Unit 5 — Following a Story
- Scenario: Watching anime or reading manga without subtitles
- Skills: inferring meaning from context, reading mood from tone
- Common plot phrases: 〜のせいで (because of), 〜はずだ (should be), 〜かもしれない (might be)
- Practice: pick a clip or chapter and work through it together

## Phase 4: Kanji Progression

Kanji woven throughout Phases 2 and 3, not taught in isolation.

| Stage | Target | Example |
|-------|--------|---------|
| Beginner | 50 kanji | 日、月、人、大、小、食、駅、円 |
| Elementary | 150 kanji | Common signs, menus, transit |
| Pre-intermediate | 300 kanji | Simple manga, game menus |

Method: Learn kanji in the scenario where it naturally appears. See 食 first in the restaurant unit, not in a list.

## Lesson Format (Template)

Every lesson follows the same structure:

1. **Scenario** — Set the scene. What's happening, where are you, what do you need to do?
2. **Key vocab** — 5–10 words, always with example sentences in context
3. **Grammar point** — One pattern per lesson, explained simply with examples
4. **Dialogue** — A short exchange using the lesson's vocab and grammar
5. **Practice** — Fill-in, translation, or role-play exercise
6. **Culture note** — One insight that makes the language make sense
7. **Review** — 5-question quiz on lesson content
